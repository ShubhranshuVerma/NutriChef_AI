# Cooking basics

## Tempering (tadka / chaunk)
Whole spices such as cumin or mustard seeds are fried briefly in hot oil or ghee until they crackle, then poured over dals, raitas or vegetables. Add curry leaves and garlic after the seeds so they do not burn.

## Building a gravy (masala)
Most North Indian gravies start by cooking onion until golden, then ginger-garlic, then tomato and ground spices until the oil separates. Blending the base gives a smooth gravy without cream.

## Cooking pulses
- Moong dal and masoor dal cook quickly (about 15-20 minutes simmering) and usually need no soaking.
- Toor dal and chana dal cook faster when soaked for 30-60 minutes; a pressure cooker needs about 3-4 whistles.
- Chickpeas and rajma need overnight soaking and thorough pressure cooking until completely soft.

## Keeping protein high without extra fat
- Use paneer, eggs, dals, sprouts, tofu or soya chunks as the main ingredient rather than a garnish.
- Grill, pan-roast or scramble instead of deep frying.
- Measure oil and ghee; small amounts add many calories.

## Using what is already in the kitchen
- Leftover rice becomes curd rice, lemon rice or fried rice.
- Softening tomatoes suit gravies, dal and chutneys.
- Wilting greens can be blended into palak dishes, dal or chilla batter.
- Plan meals so perishable items (leafy greens, paneer, curd, mushrooms) are used first.

## Portions
A typical home portion is about 150-200 g of cooked dal or sabzi, 1 cup (about 150-180 g) of cooked rice, or 2 medium chapatis.
