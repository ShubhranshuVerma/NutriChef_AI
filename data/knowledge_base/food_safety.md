# Food safety basics

## Safe minimum internal temperatures (USDA FSIS)
- Poultry (whole or ground): 74 C / 165 F.
- Ground meats: 71 C / 160 F.
- Beef, pork, lamb and veal steaks, chops and roasts: 63 C / 145 F, then rest for 3 minutes.
- Fish and shellfish: 63 C / 145 F.
- Egg dishes: 71 C / 160 F; cook eggs until yolk and white are firm.
- Leftovers and casseroles: 74 C / 165 F.

## Temperature control
- Keep the refrigerator at or below 4 C / 40 F.
- The "danger zone" where bacteria grow fastest is 4-60 C / 40-140 F.
- Do not leave perishable food out for more than 2 hours (1 hour if the surroundings are above 32 C / 90 F).

## Pulses and beans
- Raw or undercooked red kidney beans (rajma) contain phytohaemagglutinin, which can cause severe stomach upset. Soak them for at least 5 hours, discard the water, and boil them vigorously for at least 10 minutes (pressure cooking is also effective). Slow cooking at low temperatures is not enough.
- Rinse dals and pulses before cooking and pick out stones.

## Dairy and eggs
- Use pasteurised milk and paneer from reliable sources; refrigerate paneer and use it within a few days.
- Avoid raw or runny eggs for young children, pregnant people, older adults and people with weakened immunity.

## Hygiene
- Wash hands, boards and knives after handling raw meat, poultry, fish or eggs.
- Keep raw foods separate from ready-to-eat foods.
- Wash fruit and vegetables under running water, especially leafy greens and sprouts.
